This post is about the economic consequences when we consider the soul as capital in economic models. The theoretical paper is in the Book link on this blog site.

# A missing element in economics

Most economic models neglect the soul. Perhaps it is done on purpose because economics mainly starts from a testable hypothesis that we can take to the data. The soul, if it exists, is a latent variable. For those that do not believe that a soul exists a model can be kept tractable and mostly observable without relaying on an unobserved component (the exception is total factor productivity).

In neoclassical economics we measure investments in human and physical capital and how it accumulates into a stock of human and physical capital. These assets (the mind and tangible things) enter our supply function and describes how we make stuff. We need materials and we need humans and machines. Not much to be contested at this stage.

We do not really ask what a human is, apart from the body used for moving stuff around, assembling things or clicking on keyboards. We think we have a mind. The human being invests in keeping a body healthy and learns things to do things better or to satisfy curiousity. Obviously some humans neglect their health...

In the paper, I argue that a human also has a soul. This soul matters for how we invest in the mind and the body. Not everyone accepts this premise. I cannot prove to you that a soul exists. However, I take it by faith that it exists (more on this later).

What I do need to show, however, is that if my assumption holds, does everything else that follows from that assumption also hold (e.g., a set of propositions). 

# Why bother?

I suspect the soul is the varible that transforms our behaviour. It directs our will. It interacts with the mind and the soul. If it does these things, then we can learn something about how are economies move when we operate under different soul states (high vs. low).

During the next few posts I will try to show that a soul state can describe why two different countries with the same institutions may produce different economic environments. I will show how an economy can get stuck in a low equilibrium when the soul state is below a critical threshold. I.e., the main hypothesis is that a high soul state must lead to better economic conditions, justice and an improvement in welfare.

# Grace is behind a high soul state

The model's innovation is exogenous grace. This may not sound like much, but most of economics is spent on identification. Identification of shocks, parameters and processes. If all processes are endogenous to a system we will struggle to understand the system's internal dynamics and mechanisms. We use the language of mathematics to demonstrate things like stability and equilibrium.

Most of the growth literature centers on explaining total factor productivity. In many models this is a residual (the difference between outputs and inputs). Systematically, researchers attempt to endogenize components of this variable (e.g., R&D expenditures on patents to receive the rents of innovations, ladder models of technological change, churning in models of competition etc.). 

I argue that grace is a truly exogenous variable, at least as experienced by humans. If humans cannot manufacture it (which I will try to argue), then it has to come from the outside. In my model, this is supplied by God. My identification strategy is theological, specifically the Protestant variety of Christianity (bare with me).

# Two simple equations

The paper is quite technical and there are several propositions. However, most of what is new (over the standard neoclassical model) can be summarized by two equations: a state equation that describes the soul state and a grace equation that explains how grace operates in the system.

Let's unpack the soul state first:

$$S_t =(1-\delta) S_{t-1}+\phi^S (i_t^S)+\Xi \bar{S}+\mathbf{G}_t-\rho \theta_t$$

This equation says that the individual's soul state today $t$ equals the soul state yesterday $S_{t-1}$, depreciates with $\delta$, benefits from networks (or the soul state of the community $\bar{S}$) by $\Xi$, increases with devotional investment $i_t^S$ by $\phi^S$. It rises with grace $\mathbf{G}_t$ and decreases with dishonesty $\rho \theta_t$. Let's take each of them in turn:

- With no devotional invesment, not grace and no dishonesty, the state of the soul can diminish. Earthly pleasures, partaking in things that are harmful can lead to a lower soul state.
- Just like we invest in human capital and taking care of the body, so too can we take care of the soul through prayer and medidation. Later on we will determine what the shadow value of this devotion looks like relative to the shadow value of investing the mind and in the body.
- It is often easier to grow spiritually if the community embraces spirituality. Think of a church, or a group of people that go to yoga. $\Xi$ is just a measure that tells us how much we benefit from existing networks. 
- The dishonesty variable makes the heart hard. It is a corrosion variable. It gives us a cost of corruption.
- Grace is exogenous. See below. 

It is important to note that the soul state variable resembles sanctification (the transformation of character and desire progressively orientated towards God) and not justification (whether a believer is declared righteous). 

Next I define the process for grace. Grace, from the human experience is exogenous. I do not know the timing or even if I will receive grace. Grace from what? In Christianty we receive faith and God grace and forgiveness of sins. In previous blog posts I talk about sin quite a lot. Christ is the perfect substitute for the sinner to carry God's wrath. I.e., through Christ, justice and forgiveness is possible. 

We write $\mathbf{G}_t$ as:

$$\mathbf{G}_t = \bar{G}+\varepsilon_t^G$$

Grace has two elements. There is common grace $(\bar{G})$, which is given universally and does not require any faith. This is God's persistent kindness, His sustaining of all life and his restraining of absolute evil (we don't go around hunting each other).  It is the reason that even thoroughly secular societies retain a residual moral sense, that cooperation does not collapse entirely, and that beauty persists in a fallen world.

The second element represents effectual grace $(\varepsilon_t^G \geq 0)$. It is the specific, transformative work of the Holy Spirit that regenerates the heart and grants the gift of faith. Effectual grace produces conversion, revival, and awakening. It is particular, given to those whom God has chosen before the foundation of the world (Ephesians 1:4), and it is irresistible in the sense that when God purposes to transform a soul, the transformation occurs.

Four of its features deserve emphaisis:

- **Exogeneity:** People cannot manufacture it, earn it or control it. It is a gift.
- **Common grace is persistent and universal:** Even if people do not invest in the soul, the steady state of the soul is not zero (i.e., the soul state does not necessarily collapse to nothing). This means that there is a grace floor where God sustains a minimal spiritual awareness and conscience even it the most negligent person. It is the element that keeps evil in check.
- **Effectual grace is particular and stochastic:** It is not given to every one. God regenerates who He wants. What makes this grace is that while all have turned away from God, God chooses to save some, not by their effort, but by God's grace, and chooses to leave others in their will, which is turned against God.
- **Non-negativity:** Effectual grace, while stochastic to the one receiving it is always positive (a gift, never a punishment). While God allows for spiritual decay, He never actively destroys the soul.

The paper goes into some length to describe overlaps between common grace and the natural law. For now we leave that for the interested reader.
