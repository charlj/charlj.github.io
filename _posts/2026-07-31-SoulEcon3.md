In the previous posts I described the soul state within economic models. In the second post I spent time trying to position Christianity's (from a Protestant perspective) unique position in providing grace that transforms us. In this post we will explore some propositions and look at simulations from the model.

## Shadow values

Economic models are mostly derived from first principles. That is, we build optimization models with specific objectives. As an example, households will want to maximize utility subject to some constraints, or firms minimize costs subject to constraints. The optimization problems deliver first order conditions. When our models are dynamic and when we take optimization constraints into account we get a set of results that help us determine optimal allocations (e.g., how much to consume vs. spend on leisure) as well as shadow values (or prices).

A shadow value is the price of something that is never traded. Nobody quotes it and it appears in no account. It is what one more unit of a scarce thing is worth to the agent at the optimum, so that if we relax a constraint by one unit, the shadow value tells us how much better off the agent is. The price of a loaf of bread is on the shelf. The value to me of one more year of health, or one more year of schooling, is not. The shadow value tells me what that worth is.

Our model carries four shadow values. $\lambda_t$ is the value of one more rand of income today. $\mu^B_t$ is the value of one more unit of the body, $\mu^M_t$ one more unit of the mind, and $\mu^S_t$ one more unit of the soul. The first prices a flow, the other three price stocks: what the agent's whole remaining future is worth, at the margin, for having a little more body, mind, or soul in it. None of them is observed. All of them are decided inside the model.

## A shadow price at work: the decision to cheat

The quickest way to see a shadow price do something is to watch the agent decide whether to steal. Recall from the last post that a person can skim $\theta_t \Pi_t$ from the firm where they work. Cheating pays: one more unit of $\theta$ raises income by $\Pi_t$, and income is worth $\lambda_t$, so the gain is $\lambda_t \Pi_t$. Cheating also corrodes: it takes $\rho \theta_t$ off the soul, and the soul is worth $\mu^S_t$, so the cost is $\mu^S_t \rho$. Both sides are linear in $\theta$, so the agent does not shave at the margin. The two are simply compared:

$$\mu^S_t \rho > \lambda_t \Pi_t \; \Rightarrow \; \theta_t = 0, \qquad \mu^S_t \rho < \lambda_t \Pi_t \; \Rightarrow \; \theta_t = \bar{\theta}(1-S_t)$$

Note what the first case says. The agent is *permitted* to cheat. The moral ceiling is slack, nobody is watching, and the agent declines anyway, because the soul that would be damaged is worth more than the rents on offer. That is honesty chosen rather than honesty enforced. The distance between the two is the distance between conscience and compliance. Where $\mu^S_t$ sits near zero the comparison runs the other way and the agent takes everything the ceiling allows. Notice also that no policeman appears anywhere in the condition.

## The equilibrium across body, mind and soul

Investment has to be split three ways. We eat and exercise, we study, we pray. Every rand can only go to one of them. The first order conditions say that the agent keeps shifting rands until no further gain is available:

$$\mu^B_t \phi^{B^{'}}(i^B_t) = \mu^M_t \frac{\partial \phi^M}{\partial i^M_t} = \mu^S_t \phi^{S^{'}}(i^S_t) = \lambda_t$$

$\lambda_t$ is what a rand is worth if it is simply spent, which is the price of not investing at all. Each of the other three terms is what a rand buys if it is invested instead: how much of the stock that rand produces, which is the marginal product $\phi^{\prime}$, times what a unit of that stock is worth, which is the shadow value $\mu$. Equality is the equilibrium. If devotion bought more at the margin than the gym, the agent would pray more and lift less, which would push the return to prayer down and the return to the gym up until the two met. The mechanism is not exotic. It is the same arbitrage that equalizes returns across any two assets.

That is the point I want to press, because it is easy to miss. Devotion is not piety sitting outside the budget constraint, admired and unpriced. It is an investment, denominated in the same units as a semester of school or a month of physiotherapy, and it wins or loses the rand on exactly the same terms. There is no separate holy account. Someone who prays an hour a day has bought that hour with something, and the model says what.

The condition also tells us where to look next. Three of the four terms are ordinary: $\mu^B$ and $\mu^M$ are the standard shadow values of health and human capital, and $\lambda$ is the standard marginal utility of income. Everything new is in $\mu^S_t$.

## What makes the soul valuable

Mathematically the shadow value of the soul is:

$$\mu^S_t = \underbrace{\beta ^{'} (S_{t+1}) V_{t+1}}_{\text{patience}}+\beta(S_{t+1}) \left[\underbrace{\frac{\partial u_{t+1}}{\partial \sigma} \frac{\partial \sigma}{\partial S}}_{\text{satiation}} +\underbrace{\frac{\partial u_{t+1}}{\partial \psi} \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial S}}_{\text{generosity}} -\underbrace{\nu_{t+1} \bar{\theta}}_{\text{honesty}} +\mu_{t+1}^S (1-\delta^S)\right]$$

where $u_{t+1}$ is next period's utility and $V_{t+1}$ is the continuation value, meaning everything the agent has left to live for from $t+1$ onward. The equation looks complicated, but it is only the four channels of the last post priced and added up. The soul is the *meta* variable that transforms preferences, time horizons, social orientation and moral constraint simultaneously, and each brace is one of those transformations valued at the margin.

The patience term is positive: a higher soul raises the discount factor applied to the entire future, scaling up everything the agent still has to live for. The generosity term is positive. The satiation term is a level effect whose sign depends on the units of consumption. The honesty term enters negatively when the moral ceiling binds: at the margin, a higher soul tightens conscience and withholds rents the agent still wanted. Conscience is privately costly exactly when temptation is still alive, and socially productive all the while. The term also vanishes for a high-S agent, who no longer wishes to cheat $(\nu = 0)$, and drags hardest at low S, so the private return to the soul is weakest where society is trapped.

$\mu^S_t$ is small when $S$ is small. A soul near empty is not worth much at the margin: patience has barely begun to move, there is no generosity to price, and the honesty term is dragging hardest of all. Now put that small number back into the investment condition above. The marginal product of devotion would have to be enormous to make a rand of prayer beat a rand of anything else, and it is not, so the agent rationally invests nothing in the soul at all. The social multiplier makes it worse still, because $\mu^S_t$ is low precisely when everyone else's $\bar{S}$ is low too, and so no one has a reason to move first. That is the poverty trap of the soul. It is also why the model needs grace to arrive from outside rather than be bought from within.

## Three further results

Apart from patience, generosity, honesty and investing in the mind, the model makes three further predictions:

- Forgiveness equilibrium: One of the important reasons for working on this paper and blog was to highlight how a soul may lead to different revenge-forgiveness equilibria. Specifically, if one group of people in a nation treats another group poorly, how should the affected group react when the balance of power shifts? In a simple game-theoretic framework where our options are forgive-forgive, forgive-revenge, revenge-forgive and revenge-revenge, the gain to forgiving rises with the soul state, and it crosses zero at a threshold that the baseline calibration puts at $S^F \approx 0.28$. Above the threshold, forgiveness is strictly dominant regardless of who moves first, and mutual forgiveness is the unique equilibrium. Below it I should be careful about what actually fails, because it is not what one expects. Revenge is never literally dominant. What collapses is *mutual* forgiveness: forgiving pays only if the other side forgives too, so every equilibrium below the threshold leaves at least one group taking revenge while the group that forbears absorbs the blow. Deeper wounds are worse again, since they open a region where each group revenges only because it expects the other to, and the expectation makes itself true.
- Unemployment: Unemployment typically rises in models where unions bargain for higher wages for its members. This happens because the bargained wage is typically a markup over the productivity wage (i.e., the wage that equals a worker's true productive effort). If wages are higher than the marginal productivity of labor then demand for work can fall below supply leading to rising unemployment. However, in a model with a soul state both sides of the bargain change. A union whose members care for the well-being of others internalizes the cost that an aggressive wage demand imposes on the outsiders who never get hired, and moderates its markup. A firm whose owners have a high soul state takes a smaller price markup over cost. Structural unemployment rises in both markups, so it falls in the average soul state.
- Critical mass: the model has two stable equilibria with an unstable one between them. Write the steady state as replenishment against decay. Replenishment is grace, plus what devotional investment produces, minus what cheating corrodes, and it is S-shaped in the average soul state: flat at the bottom because nobody invests, steep in the middle where the complementarity engages and corruption recedes, flat again at the top where returns diminish. A straight decay line crosses an S-shaped curve three times. Under the baseline calibration a society settles at one of the outer two and tips at the middle one:

$$\bar{S}_L \approx 0.20, \qquad \bar{S}^{*} \approx 0.65, \qquad \bar{S}_H \approx 0.85$$

The tipping point is the sobering number. Converting an average into a headcount, and holding the untransformed fixed, crossing it takes roughly two-thirds of the population. Tipping a society is not the work of a devout remnant.

## Two cities

Augustine wrote *De Civitate Dei* between 413 and 426 AD, while Rome was being sacked and pagans blamed the Christians for it. His answer was not to defend Rome. It was to say that Rome had been built on the love of self, on the *libido dominandi*, and that the same disordered desire that built it brought it down. The two cities are the two equilibria.

|  | City of Man (low equilibrium) | City of God (high equilibrium) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Driving force | Self-love (*amor sui*) | Love of God (*amor Dei*) |
| Desire | Unbounded, insatiable | Bounded by contentment |
| Time horizon | Short-term, impatient | Long-term, patient |
| Social orientation | Self-interest, exploitation | Generosity, service |
| Moral constraint | External only (law, punishment) | Internal (conscience, conviction) |
| Model parameters | Low $S$, low $\beta$, low $\sigma$, $\psi \approx 0$ | High $S$, high $\beta$, high $\sigma$, high $\psi$ |
| Corruption | High ($\theta \approx \bar{\theta}$) | Low ($\theta \approx 0$) |
| Savings | Low | High |
| Forgiveness | Unsustainable; revenge in equilibrium | Forgiveness dominant |
| Growth | Stagnant | Flourishing |

Augustine understood what the model formalizes: that the difference between a flourishing
society and a decaying one is not primarily institutional but dispositional. It depends on what
the citizens love. “Two cities have been formed by two loves: the earthly by the love of self,
even to the contempt of God; the heavenly by the love of God, even to the contempt of self”
(De Civitate Dei, XIV.28).

Kierkegaard sharpens the diagnosis. In *The Sickness Unto Death* he defines despair as the condition of a self that wills not to be the self it truly is. The self, for Kierkegaard, is constituted by a Power outside itself, and despair is the refusal of that relationship: the self tries to build itself on its own terms and becomes a fabrication, an image rather than a person. In the language of our model that is the low-$S$ agent exactly.

When $S \rightarrow 0$, the agent rejects the divine constitution of the self and builds an identity around desire (power, status, wealth). The result is not freedom but bondage: insatiable consumption $(\sigma_{low})$, impatience $(\beta_{low})$, dishonesty $(\theta_{high})$, and indifference to others $(\psi \approx 0)$. The agent becomes less than human, not more. And when an entire society is composed of such agents, it is easy to see why it turns to evil, that ultimately destroys itself. The low-S equilibrium is in Kierkegaard’s sense, a society in despair: a collective of selves that have rejected the Power that constituted them, and in doing so have lost the capacity to flourish.

## Putting numbers on it

The two columns above are qualitative. The paper calibrates them. The parameters take standard values from the macro literature where they exist (a discount factor between 0.92 and 0.98, a labor share of 0.65) and plausible ones where they do not (spiritual decay at 0.30, a social multiplier of 0.10). Nothing here is estimated. The numbers illustrate a magnitude. They do not measure the world.

With that said, the magnitudes are worth seeing. Moving a society from the low equilibrium to a soul state just past the tipping point:

| Outcome | Low $S$ ($\approx 0.20$) | High $S$ ($\approx 0.70$) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dishonesty rate $\theta$ | 24% | 9% |
| Giving as a share of consumption | ~5% | ~25% |
| Investment share of income | ~7% | ~25% |
| Measured TFP (fraction of true) | 84% | 94% |
| GDP (index, low $S$ = 100) | 100 | ~175 |
| Forgiveness game | Revenge in equilibrium | Forgiveness dominant |

Two things about the table are worth dwelling on. The first is that the gains change composition as $S$ rises. At low soul states the honesty dividend dominates, because a fast way to make a poor society richer is to stop the stealing. At moderate soul states human capital takes over as patient agents start investing in education. At high soul states it is plain capital accumulation, compounding on a high savings rate. The second is the timing. When the model is run as a transition rather than compared across steady states, a revival starting from the low equilibrium delivers about 30% more GDP over sixty periods, well short of the 75% in the table, because the soul state climbs only partway and human capital adjusts to greater patience with a lag. The transition takes a generation.

The magnitude should give us pause. No tax incentive, no regulatory framework and no constitutional amendment produces effects of this size. The model says what Augustine said: the binding constraint on a society is not its institutions but the disposition of its citizens' hearts.
