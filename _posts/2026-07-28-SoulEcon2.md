In the previous post I discussed two equations that are added to the standard economic model to describe the soul. In this post I will primarily focus on why Christianity is uniquely positioned to provide the type of grace that transforms the human.

## What types of transformation are we talking about?

A few transformations happen. There are seven propositions that a later blog will describe. For now I will focus on four transformations relevant to economics. 

- Grace transforms a person to become more patient: The time discount factor changes. Without the soul the discount factor is simply $\underline{\beta}$. However with the soul state the parameter becomes endogenous such that $\beta (S_t) = \underline{\beta}+(\bar{\beta} - \underline{\beta}) S_t$, where $\beta$ rises with the soul state. The higher one's soul state is, the more patient an individual becomes. This leads to several interesting properties in economic models. The first is that consumption smoothing increases - or delaying gratification. It changes the returns to assets and eventually leads to larger rates of investment into capital (both physical and human).
- Grace leads to higher satisfaction from having less: The curvature of utility changes, which is the inverse of the intertemporal elasticity of substitution. It becomes $\sigma (S_t) = \underline{\sigma}+(\bar{\sigma} - \underline{\sigma}) S_t$ where a higher soul state leads to a larger parameter. This changes a few things in the model. It changes the marginal rate of substitution between consumption and work or leisure. It prompts people to save more and to work more.
- Grace makes a person more generous: The model adds generosity into a person's utility function (nothing novel here - this has been done in the altruism literature). Generosity in the utility function is defined as $\psi (S) \frac{g_t^{1-\sigma (S)}}{1-\sigma (S)}$. $\psi$ is a preference shifter. It tells us how much an individual prefers to give. With a higher soul state this preference increases.
- Grace makes a person more honest: If one can steal or cheat without any consequences then an individual may do so optimally at a limit (it is bad for society, but beneficial at an individual level). In the person's budget constraint a person can "steal" $\theta_t \Pi$ from the firm where they work. Our constraint is $0 \leq \theta_t \leq \bar{\theta} (1-S)$. The higher the soul state, the lower one will want to cheat or steal. From an economics perspective this leads to a higher social welfare gain. I.e., people around you benefit from moral behavior.


## Why is Christianity uniquely positioned compared to other religions?

It is true that grace is supplied by other religions too. Let me briefly describe how before making my case for Christianity. Also, I would be glad if my readers can add to or correct what I state. If there are errors I would be happy to correct them too.

In **Judaism** grace is related to God's covenant to Israel. God chose Israel “not because you were more in number than any other people,” but because He loved them (Deuteronomy 7:7–8). Israel's actions did not merit anything. Exactly the opposite, God remained faithful to Israel in spite of a long history of disobedience and disbelief. God showed Israel favor when He led them out of bondage in Egypt, when He led them through the desert and provided for their every need and when He defeated the nations. Yet the moral transformation of the agent runs through repentance (teshuvah), which the tradition holds to be within the agent’s power, divinely assisted rather than divinely given: “Return to me, and I will return to you” (Malachi 3:7); “one who comes to purify himself is helped” (b. Shabbat 104a). Note here that this is a *cooperative case* of grace.

**Islam** emphasizes submission to God's law and the Five Pillars, with deeds weighed on the scales (mīzān) at judgment. This should not be overstated as salvation “earned”: Islam holds a strong doctrine of divine mercy (raḥma) and bounty (faḍl). The tradition insists that none enters Paradise by deeds alone but only by God’s mercy, and Ashʿarī theology is strongly predestinarian. What is effort-driven is the moral transformation of the agent in this life, even if acquittal at judgment ultimately rests on mercy. In our model, common grace $\bar{G}$ remains but effectual grace is absent ($\varepsilon_t^G = 0$), with a high return on devotional investment. However, there is no exogenous breakthrough, with divine mercy entering at *judgment* rather than an infused transformation of the soul.

**Buddhism** (in its Theravāda and Zen forms) teaches that liberation is realized through the Eightfold Path, right view, right intention, and so on, with the emphasis on self-effort (jiriki, “self-power”), mindfulness, and the reduction of craving (taṇhā) rather than desire as such. Here effectual grace is zero, with an objective that minimizes craving rather than transforming it. The important exception is Pure Land Buddhism (Jōdo Shū, Jōdo Shinshū), whose tariki (“other-power”) soteriology rests entirely on Amida’s vow; in Shinran even entrusting faith (shinjin) is Amida’s gift, not the practitioner’s work. Pure Land thus instantiates the pure-exogeneity case within Buddhism. This is grace given by a vow of a human, who through eons accumulated good karma and willed it to those who have faith. This sounds similar to Christianity, with the difference being that Amida is not a God (he is not a Creator and does not judge human sin). His "other-power" is accumulated by works. Believers **rely** on this "other-power". It is spiritual currency (believers borrow from it). What are you being saved from? Samsara - the endless loop of dying, losing loved ones and risking rebirth in lower realms (such as the animal, ghost or Naraka realm). Rebirth takes place in Pure Land, making it safer and easier to achieve Nirvana.

**Hinduism** offers multiple paths (yogas) - devotion, knowledge, action, meditation; each directed at the individual’s effort toward mokṣa. In the effort-based strands (jñāna and karma yoga, and the “monkey-hold” (markaṭa-nyāya) reading of devotion) the devotee’s love is the precondition of divine favor, and the mechanism is endogenous. But this must be qualified. The Teṅkalai school of Śrī Vaiṣṇavism develops a doctrine of “grace without a cause” (nirhetuka-kṛpā). This is the “cat-hold” (mārjāra-nyāya), in which the kitten is carried passively and grace is entirely unearned and prior to the devotee’s love, anchored in Bhagavad-Gītā 18.66. This southern strand is close to the pure-exogeneity case, and we note it as the bhakti tradition’s nearest analogue to unmerited grace. Once again, one is saved from Samsara with the goal of being free from *cosmic bondage* and entering the spiritual realm of Vishnu. In this tradition the goddess Lakshmi intercedes for people as a divine mediator. Lakshmi represents divine mercy while Vishnu represents justice. When a soul turns to Lakshmi she pleads (with love) the person's case to Vishnu to overlook past sins (karma) and grant unmerited grace. The principle of letting go is a one-time commitment that is a spiritual and psychological pivot. Note that Vishnu absorbs the sins rather than requiring that they be paid for. I.e., there is no atonement.

In **Secular Humanism** moral progress relies on human reason, education and institutional design. The soul is assumed away and the burden of improvement rests on the mind. 

## Two problems that grace must solve

**Christianity** is different from other religions in ways that matter for the economics. While grace is the model's mechanism, the cause starts centrally on Christ. Grace is not a freestanding force that the world’s religions parameterize differently; it is a gift that flows from a person and from what that person did. In Christianity the nature of a human is broken. All have sinned (Romans 3:23) and sin is defined as the will set against God's will (1 John 3:4). From Christ grace flows to transform the will of the human and orient it towards God's will. Salvation (from an eternal hell) and moral transformation is a gift from God. “For it is by grace you have been saved, through faith—and this is not from yourselves, it is the gift of God—not by works, so that no one can boast” (Ephesians 2:8–9).

What makes Christianity unique is that the atonement and imputed righteousness have no parallel. I should be plain about which Christianity I mean. Imputation, righteousness reckoned to the sinner rather than infused into him, is the Reformation reading, and Rome and Orthodoxy would both contest it. My identification strategy is Protestant, as I said last time.

Grace solves two problems that the other religions do not solve. The first is the corruption that we all inherit from the beginning of Genesis. The will failed by inverting itself, preferring the self to God. A broken will cannot re-will itself. Humans do not have the *power* to change themselves. If a human only has a broken will, then only brokenness will emerge from an attempt to bootstrap oneself out of hell.

The second is *guilt*. God's justice is non-negotiable. Guilt must be satisfied. This is where atonement enters. Christ takes up the sinner's guilt and is punished for it. Christ, in the Christian tradition, is not merely a man or a demi-god, but is God. He is not separate from God or a second god. He is God.

In Islam the justice problem is in full force and the scales are important. No one can bear the burden of an individual. Justice is resolved by a pardon. In Pure Land, there is transfer without a judge. Amida's merit is entrusted to the believer. There is no holy person whose justice is at stake. Teṅkalai grace is compassion overriding karma, with neither satisfaction nor transfer.

Christianity alone holds both. There is a justice that must be satisfied rather than waived, so that God may be “just and the justifier of the one who has faith in Jesus” (Romans 3:26), and there is a transfer that satisfies it, the imputation of an alien righteousness: “God made him who had no sin to be sin for us, so that in him we might become the righteousness of God” (2 Corinthians 5:21). The transfer completes the chain. In one garden the first Adam’s will broke; in another garden, Gethsemane, the second Adam’s held: “not my will, but yours be done” (Luke 22:42). Paul draws the parallel: “through the disobedience of the one man the many were made sinners, so also through the obedience of the one man the many will be made righteous” (Romans 5:19). What is imputed is the obedience the test required and the first will failed to give. The transaction is anchored in a datable public event, a historical atonement.

Rabbinic Judaism answers the two problems differently. The power problem it declines: in place of a corrupted nature stand two inclinations, the yetzer ha-ra and the yetzer ha-tov. The agent is charged to master the first (“sin is crouching at your door; it desires to have you, but you must rule over it,” Genesis 4:7, with Deuteronomy 30:14 read the same way: “the word is very near you”). With no broken faculty, repentance within the agent’s power is coherent, and teshuvah, joined to prayer and charity, replaces the altar as the means of atonement. The guilt problem it holds in full force, before the same holy judge, but the verdict is annual: written on Rosh Hashanah, sealed on Yom Kippur, and reopened the next year. There is transfer language at the margin, the merit of the fathers (zekhut avot) invoked in the liturgy, but no imputation that settles the agent’s standing once for all.

## Three commitments

Consider three commitments. *Justice*: sin is an objective liability before a holy judge, to be satisfied rather than waived. *Inability*: the corrupted agent can neither repair the faculty nor discharge the liability; this is the first problem extended to payment. *Assurance*: the agent’s standing can be settled now, once for all, within the mortal horizon. Taking all three means that someone else must pay, and the payment must be counted as the agent’s own. Substitution and imputation are the unique solution to the system. Every rival tradition relaxes at least one commitment instead. Works-piety, inside and outside Christianity, relaxes inability: the agent pays down the debt himself. Islam relaxes assurance: the verdict waits for the scales. Rabbinic Judaism relaxes both at once: teshuvah lies within the agent’s power, and the verdict is renewed each year. Pure Land and Teṅkalai relax justice: the debt is carried or overridden, not paid.

The whole comparison collapses into one grid. *Held* means the tradition keeps the commitment; *relaxed* means it gives the commitment up in order to keep the other two.

| Tradition | Justice | Inability | Assurance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Works-piety (inside and outside Christianity) | held | **relaxed** | held |
| Islam | held | held | **relaxed** |
| Rabbinic Judaism | held | **relaxed** | **relaxed** |
| Pure Land, Teṅkalai | **relaxed** | held | held |
| Christianity | held | held | held |

Only the last row needs a substitute.

## Back to the model

Why does a doctrine about justification matter for a model of sanctification? In the last post I was careful to separate the two. The soul state $S_t$ tracks sanctification, the slow reorientation of desire, and not justification, the verdict on the person. The two equations still need the verdict.

Take the grace process $\mathbf{G}_t = \bar{G}+\varepsilon_t^G$. It is exogenous only if grace is not bought. Where transformation is earned, effectual grace becomes a function of devotional investment, $\varepsilon_t^G = f(i_t^S)$, and the innovation collapses back into the state equation. There is no shock left to identify. Every tradition that relaxes inability does exactly this.

Now take assurance. If the verdict is annual, or waits for the scales, then $i_t^S$ is a payment against an outstanding debt, and the shadow value of prayer is the shadow price of a liability priced by fear. Under imputation the debt is settled and $i_t^S$ buys nothing. That is what makes the return to devotion a return to transformation rather than a return to a purchase, and it is why the same devotional effort can produce a different soul state in two otherwise identical economies.
