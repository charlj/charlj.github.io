## About the Book

South Africa has so much potential, and yet so little of it has been realized. This book is an economics treatise that moves from **diagnosis** to **prescription** to **foundation** --- confronting the fundamental flaws in how we diagnose and resolve South Africa's problems.

The argument is straightforward: South Africa's economic decline is not primarily a resource problem but a **policy and soul problem**. Free-market reform grounded in Christian ethics --- honesty, forgiveness, servitude, and stewardship --- can reverse it.

This is not prosperity gospel. It is an economist's honest conviction that the data points to virtue, and virtue points to God.

## Chapter Overview

### Part I: Diagnosis --- Growth and Labor

**Introduction: Do What Is Right**
The book begins by acknowledging South Africa's history of injustice --- colonialism, apartheid, exploitation --- and asking the hard question: can one correct for an evil history? The answer requires us to move beyond revenge and toward policies that actually work.

**Chapter 1: Why Do We Care About Economic Growth?**
Growth is not an abstract target --- it is the mechanism through which employment rises, poverty falls, and opportunity expands. This chapter confronts the misguided narrative that growth objectives are themselves misguided, comparing Marxist, Neo-Classical, and Post-Keynesian perspectives with South African evidence.

**Chapter 2: What Drives Economic Growth?**
Capital, labor, total factor productivity, and intermediate inputs --- South Africa underperforms on all of them. The chapter traces why: poor education, electricity failures, overregulation, and a collapse in trade openness. TFP decline is framed as fundamentally an honesty problem.

**Chapter 3: The Labor Market**
Unemployment at catastrophic levels. Youth unemployment above 60%. Wages decoupled from productivity. A Nash bargaining framework shows how union power, high reservation wages, and rigid labor laws create a structural ratchet where unemployment not only stays high but *rises over time*.

### Part II: Diagnosis --- Government

**Chapter 4: Government Functions and Outcomes**
What should governments do, and what should they not? South Africa's government is too large, too corrupt, and too inefficient. Debt is unsustainable. The tax burden falls on a shrinking base. The chapter introduces a taxpayer-weighted social welfare function and asks: who should the government actually represent?

**Chapter 5: Governing Well**
Why has governance collapsed? Corruption, cadre deployment, and a leadership crisis have cost hundreds of billions of rands in SOE bailouts and load-shedding. Data from the Auditor General, the Zondo Commission, and governance indicators paint a stark picture of deterioration since 2008.

### Part III: Warning

**Chapter 6: How Does a Nation Turn from Its Evil Ways?**
South Africa's unfinished racial reckoning. The failure of the Truth and Reconciliation Commission. The vicious cycle of BEE as an economics of unforgiveness. What Rwanda's Gacaca courts --- 12,000 courts processing 1.2 million cases --- teach us about genuine forgiveness and national healing.

**Chapter 7: The Signs and Worries of Genocide**
A chapter most economics books would not dare to write. Using a Bayesian framework (central estimate: 41%), the chapter documents normalized hatred, a broken justice system, and presidential rhetoric that has shifted from reconciliation to silence. A provocation and a wake-up call.

### Part IV: Prescription

**Chapter 8: A New Social Compact?**
Practical reform: real-time democratic accountability, taxpayer voice in expenditure, transparent resource allocation. Drawing on lessons from Estonia, Switzerland, Brazil, and South Korea. The country reimagined as a company in which every citizen is an equity holder.

**Chapter 9: Man in Search of a Soul**
The capstone. No institutional reform will hold unless the people within those institutions are transformed. The **Trichotomous Agent Model** --- body, mind, and soul --- shows that patience, satiation, generosity, and honesty are not soft virtues but hard economic variables. Two stable equilibria exist: a low-soul trap of corruption and hatred, and a high-soul flourishing built on stewardship and grace. South Africa is stuck in the first. This book argues it can reach the second.

## The Trichotomous Agent Model

The book's formal contribution to economics. Three state variables --- **Body (B)**, **Mind (M)**, **Soul (S)** --- where the soul *transforms the utility function* rather than entering as just another argument. Four channels connect spiritual capital to economic outcomes:

| Channel | High Soul (Christ-centered) | Low Soul (desire-driven) |
|---|---|---|
| **Patience** | Long-term thinking, stewardship | Short-termism, corruption |
| **Satiation** | Easily satisfied, saves more | Insatiable, consumes all |
| **Generosity** | Voluntary giving | Pure self-interest |
| **Honesty** | Cannot cheat | No moral constraint |

When the soul parameter equals zero, the model reduces exactly to standard *homo economicus*. The model **extends** mainstream economics rather than rejecting it.

Grace is modeled as exogenous --- God's persistent offer plus stochastic breakthroughs. It cannot be earned or manufactured, analogous to technology shocks in standard macroeconomics.

Seven formal propositions connect back to every chapter of the book.

## About the Author

Charl Jooste is a Senior Economist at the World Bank. He holds a PhD in Economics from the University of Pretoria and has worked at the South African National Treasury. His research spans macroeconomic modeling, fiscal policy, and --- in this book --- the intersection of economics, governance, and faith.
